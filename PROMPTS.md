# PROMPTS

These are the prompts I used while working on the Mistvale Tea Co. assessment. I kept the requests fairly small and focused so I could make changes step by step instead of asking AI to build the whole website in one go.

## Code prompts

### 1. Clean up the existing page structure

> I’m working on the existing Mistvale Tea Co. single-page store. Please look at the current HTML/CSS/JS structure and suggest a cleaner way to organise the page without changing the product data or the overall section order. I want the code to stay easy to maintain because this is still a single HTML file.

### 2. Refine the visual system

> Help me tighten the CSS for this tea store. I want a calm premium look using the existing Mistvale colours: deep forest green, warm parchment/cream and a small saffron accent. Use Fraunces for the main display headings and Inter for UI text. Keep the spacing consistent and avoid excessive shadows, gradients or rounded cards.

### 3. Improve the header and hero section

> The header and hero currently feel a little basic. Give me a cleaner layout for the navigation, search and cart actions, and make the hero feel more like a premium tea brand. Keep the existing content and images. I also want the layout to work well on a narrow mobile screen without making the hero too tall.

### 4. Make the product listing easier to shop

> Can you improve the product card CSS and the product-grid behaviour? I need the cards to feel consistent even when product names or descriptions have different lengths. Add subtle hover/focus states, keep the image proportions consistent and make sure the grid collapses naturally on tablet and mobile.

### 5. Fix the shopping interactions

> Please review the existing JavaScript for the product search, category filter, sorting, wishlist and cart. Fix any obvious edge cases, especially quantity limits, sold-out products and stale search results. Please keep the existing simulated API approach and don't introduce a framework.

## Image prompts

### 1. Mistvale logo

> Create a simple wordmark logo for a premium Indian tea brand called “Mistvale Tea Co.” The mark should feel quiet, natural and slightly editorial. Use a clean serif wordmark with a small, understated botanical or tea-leaf detail. Forest green on a transparent or warm cream background. No badge, no cup illustration, no gradients and no extra slogan.

### 2. Hero banner

> Create a wide website hero image for Mistvale Tea Co. Show a refined morning tea scene inspired by Indian hill-grown tea: loose tea leaves, a simple ceramic cup, subtle botanical foliage and soft morning light. Use warm parchment, muted green and natural brown tones. Keep the composition uncluttered and leave generous negative space on the left side for website text. Premium editorial photography, realistic materials, no text, no logos and no people.

### 3. Assam Breakfast Black Tea — p101

> Create a clean ecommerce product photograph for “Assam Breakfast Black Tea”. Show loose dark Assam tea leaves in a small natural ceramic or wooden vessel, with a few loose leaves around it. Warm cream background, soft natural light, subtle shadow and an understated Indian tea-estate feeling. Keep the product centred with enough empty space around it for a product card. No text, labels or logos.

### 4. Darjeeling First Flush — p102

> Create a premium ecommerce product photograph for “Darjeeling First Flush”. Show delicate rolled/loose Darjeeling tea leaves with a refined tea cup or small tasting setup. The mood should suggest a fresh spring harvest from the hills: light, airy and elegant rather than dark or dramatic. Warm neutral background, soft daylight, realistic photography, minimal props and no text or branding.

### 5. Kashmiri Kahwa — p103

> Create a premium ecommerce product photograph for “Kashmiri Kahwa”. Show green tea leaves with subtle saffron strands, cardamom, cinnamon and a few almond flakes arranged naturally around the tea. Use a warm parchment background with soft morning light and a small amount of depth. Keep it elegant and uncluttered so it works inside a square product card. No text or logos.

### 6. Masala Chai Blend — p104

> Create a realistic ecommerce product photograph for “Masala Chai Blend”. Show bold black tea leaves with ginger, cardamom, cloves and black pepper as supporting ingredients. Warm cream background, soft directional light and a cosy Indian tea-shop feel without looking rustic or crowded. Keep the composition clean and premium, with the tea as the main subject. No text, labels or logos.

### 7. Nilgiri Green Tea — p105

> Create a refined ecommerce product photograph for “Nilgiri Green Tea”. Show fresh green tea leaves with a simple ceramic tea cup or small pile of leaves. The visual mood should feel fresh, cool and botanical, inspired by the Blue Mountains of South India. Use a muted warm-neutral background, natural daylight, soft shadows and plenty of negative space. No text or branding.

### 8. Chamomile & Tulsi — p106

> Create a calm ecommerce product photograph for “Chamomile & Tulsi”. Show chamomile flowers and holy basil leaves beside a light herbal tea. Keep the styling minimal and soothing, with warm cream paper, soft daylight and gentle natural shadows. It should feel premium and botanical rather than medicinal. No text, labels or logos.

### 9. Hibiscus Rose Infusion — p107

> Create a realistic ecommerce product photograph for “Hibiscus Rose Infusion”. Show deep red hibiscus petals with a few delicate rose petals and a glass or ceramic cup of ruby-coloured tea. Use a warm neutral background and soft natural light. Keep the image elegant and fresh enough to suggest that the tea can also be served iced. No text, labels or logos.

### 10. Tea Lover's Sampler Gift Box — p108

> Create a premium ecommerce product photograph for “Tea Lover’s Sampler Gift Box”. Show a tasteful wooden gift box containing six different tea selections with a simple brewing guide card. The presentation should feel like a thoughtful premium gift, not a luxury jewellery product. Warm parchment surface, soft daylight, restrained greenery and realistic materials. Keep the composition clean and centred for a product card. No extra branding or readable text.

## Notes on the image prompts

For the product images, I kept the prompts focused on the individual product rather than asking the image tool to design the whole website. The generated images were then used as the existing product assets inside the store. I also kept the product names, prices, stock information and other factual ecommerce data in code rather than asking the image model to invent them.
